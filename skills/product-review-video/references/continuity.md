# Shot selection and continuity

## Select footage for the line being spoken

Inspect actual action, eye line, product visibility, and framing. Record source file and in/out points for accepted and excluded ranges. A rejected close-up or unwanted opening outfit should not reappear when a later version is rebuilt.

- Comfort or “putting them on” narration: use a suitable sitting-down / putting-on demonstration when available, with enough time to understand the action.
- Toe shape, material, bow, sole, or finish: use a flattering, sharp detail shot. A technically relevant but unflattering foot close-up can be replaced with a clearer product view.
- Versatile styling: use outfit footage with connected movement, not an arbitrary succession of poses.
- Avoid presenter ranges that visibly look off-camera and then reset when the edit calls for a confident continuous presentation.

## Outfit changes on movement

For “turn and change outfit,” match the movement itself:

1. Locate a turn in each source and note direction, head angle, shoulder position, body scale, framing, and movement speed.
2. Cut between compatible points in the same turn direction, so the action appears to continue across outfits.
3. Prefer a precise movement-matched cut. Add a very short dissolve only if it improves the join; a long dissolve often doubles faces and shoes instead of connecting them.
4. Use restrained speed adjustment only when needed. Check hair, hands, feet, and face for interpolation artifacts.
5. Watch several seconds on both sides of every join at normal speed. A contact sheet cannot establish a smooth transition.

Do not force a turn transition if the supplied clips have no compatible motion. Choose a clean cut or another motivated join rather than inventing head movement.

## Detail zooms

Use slow eased zooms around the feature discussed. A range around 1.00–1.06 can be a useful starting point, but the source crop, sharpness, and movement determine the actual range. Avoid zooming every scene. Keep the product inside the frame, including at maximum enlargement; do not crop out the detail being described.

## Product color gallery

When narration mentions other colors, show the supplied variant images at that point. Crop store banners and unrelated promotional badges out when appropriate, or use clean cutouts if reliable tools are available. Preserve shoe contours, material, pattern, and actual colors. Do not generate undocumented colorways or claim a pattern/material variant differs only in color.

Use a consistent scale, background, spacing, and concise labels. Check that small thumbnails remain recognizable on a phone. Keep their entrance restrained and allow time to read. Do not carry unrelated store guarantees from screenshots into the edit without a reason to include them.

## Ending action: prevent the lower / raise loop

A closing shot must have a continuous source action. Reusing the same clip, reversing it, or replaying overlapping source ranges can make a product lower and then rise again unnaturally.

- Inspect the last 8–10 seconds in motion, including the join before the CTA.
- Prefer one uninterrupted usable source range with a stable hold or natural finish.
- If the range is too short, find another suitable source or carefully extend the hold. Mild retiming is acceptable only when it remains natural; inspect optical-flow artifacts. A deliberate still hold should be documented, not mistaken for moving footage.
- Do not hide the repetition with a longer dissolve. Review source in/out times as well as timeline time and ensure an accidental repeated action is actually removed.
- Preserve the approved ending when making unrelated typography or beauty edits.
