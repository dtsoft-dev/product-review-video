# Typography and subtitles

## Keep three distinct text roles

| Role | Purpose | Placement and behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | Opening idea or short editorial statement | Follow the reference hierarchy; center horizontally when requested. |
| Callout / CTA | Feature, price context, or next action | Short, tied to the visible scene, consistent with approved typography. |
| Subtitles | Actual spoken words | A fixed lower-screen anchor throughout the video. |

Changing one role does not authorize restyling all text. Preserve the latest approved choices for the other roles. Keep Vietnamese diacritics and product names correct.

## Headlines and reference matching

- Copy the user's requested wording exactly. Remove labels such as “brand · review” when asked; do not restore them in later revisions.
- Interpret “in the middle” in context. For a formerly left-aligned title, center it horizontally in the title region; do not automatically move it onto the person's face at the vertical center.
- Inspect the reference's letterforms, weight, proportions, case, spacing, line hierarchy, and color. A script initial plus serif word and sans-serif subline may require separate text spans. Do not claim an exact font match when using a visual approximation.
- When a panel makes the title look detached, remove the panel and support contrast with restrained shadow or stroke. Match light text to a dark edge treatment or dark text to a light region. Verify against every background under the title, not just the cover frame.
- Shorten ordinary headlines when authorized. Preserve exact supplied replacement text even if a previous headline was shorter. Use deliberate line breaks, not accidental wrapping.
- If the opening hook is intentionally carried by the headline, suppress duplicate opening subtitles for that interval. Resume subtitles afterward at their normal anchor. Do not remove captions globally.
- Keep appearance/disappearance gentle. Do not bounce persistent text on every word unless requested. Check animated text for clipping and overlap with faces or product features.

## Fixed subtitle placement

- Define one subtitle container and anchor for the entire composition. Do not move captions to the top on outfit shots or move them around other text. Resolve conflicts by adjusting the other text or choosing a better shot.
- Reserve a consistent height for the maximum intended lines. Use consistent line spacing and a fixed baseline strategy so one-line and two-line cues do not cause the whole caption region to jump.
- Use a readable font, a subtle contrasting outline, and a translucent backing when needed. Avoid opaque pale boxes that dominate the footage. Test contrast on light floors, dark shoes, clothing, and mixed backgrounds.
- Lower the entire subtitle region if requested, then check platform controls, caption overlays, bottom cropping, feet, and product visibility. Do not lower individual cues separately.
- Split actual speech into short readable phrases. The subtitle wording follows the voice, except for an explicitly agreed hook treatment or caption edit.

### Example from an approved 1080 × 1920 edit

These are tunable examples, not universal defaults or platform-safe guarantees:

- Subtitles: Nunito, 46 px, weight 750, line-height 1.25, white fill; 2.5 px dark stroke; dark backing at 28% opacity; restrained shadow.
- Container: 136 px high, bottom offset 285 px, with consistent alignment across all cues. Adapt the margins to the destination UI and footage.
- Opening: white “Outfit” with a Great Vibes initial **O** and Bodoni Moda 600 **utfit**; Montserrat for **OF THE DAY** and a smaller **by [brand]**. Centered title group, no panel. This approximates a reference style; it is not a required house font.
- Later callouts: warm ivory bold text, subtle dark shadow, centered horizontally. They retain their own approved style when only the opening headline changes.

Bundle font files and their licenses when permitted. Confirm fonts have loaded before capture/export and support all required characters. Keep these fonts optional; do not hardcode a brand or title into a reusable renderer.

## CTA continuity

“Choose color and size / see current price” should stay connected to the ongoing scene. If the user asks to hold the previous presenter shot, retain that shot through the CTA instead of cutting to a new shoe close-up or empty background. Keep the headline shorter than the spoken CTA where appropriate. Do not imply a past sale price is the current price.
